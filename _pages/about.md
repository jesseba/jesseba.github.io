I'm Jesseba, a Network Science PhD student at Northeastern University. I'm advised by [Dr. Sam Scarpino](https://www.networkscienceinstitute.org/people/samuel-v-scarpino). I study how learning changes networks in both the brain and machines, and use methods from network science and information theory.

In the brain, I study how the functional roles of neurons change as animals learn, and whether these changes can predict future behavior. In transformers, I look at the representations as they progress layer by layer through the residual stream and ask how the dynamics of the computations relate to the network's structure. Composition brings them together since both systems achieve generalization by recombining what they already know. This flexibility comes at a cost because a system that composes freely loses its ability to tell the inputs apart. I want to understand how each system negotiates that tradeoff, and how the coding strategy it chooses shapes what it can learn. 

Before joining Northeastern, I spent five years in [Mark Andermann's lab](https://www.andermannlab.com/) at Harvard Medical School, studying how hunger and other motivational states change what visual cortex responds to. I then worked with [Bill Lotter](https://ds.dfci.harvard.edu/our-people/bill-lotter/) at Dana-Farber Cancer Institute on how medical imaging models behave when the data they receive differs statistically from the data they were trained on. 

In my free time, I co-write [**Of Two Minds**](https://oftwominds.substack.com), a newsletter with [Grigori Guitchounts](https://www.guitchounts.com/).

When I travel I bring more camera than I need, usually to places with bad roads. Some of it [ends up here](/photography/).
